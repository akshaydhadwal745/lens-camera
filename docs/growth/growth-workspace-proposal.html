Growth Workspace: from reports to execution

Today's Growth page is a weekly report with buttons bolted on. This redesign turns it into the place where whoever owns growth sees the goal, picks up the next most valuable task, gets the AI agent to do the heavy lifting, and proves what moved the numbers.

The test it has to passA growth manager hired tomorrow logs in and, without a briefing, can answer: what is Lens trying to achieve, are we on track, what am I responsible for, what should I do next, and what did our last month of work actually achieve?

2 · Principles

Four rules the design follows

Every screen serves a growth outcome

Discovery, conversion, activation, retention, referral, revenue, partnerships. A section that can't name the outcome it moves doesn't ship. No chart galleries.

One unit of work: the task

AI recommendations, content opportunities, code proposals, partner outreach and social posts are all tasks, with one lifecycle, one owner and one success metric.

Progress is computed, never typed in

Goals move only when their metric moves; milestones move only when tasks are verified done. No hand-set percentages anywhere.

The AI does the legwork, people decide

The agent finds, explains, drafts and measures. A person approves anything that ships or costs money. Permission levels stay enforced in code.

How progress is calculated

  • Key result: (current − baseline) ÷ (target − baseline), with current read from the facts engine. Never typed by a person.
  • On track / at risk / behind: compares that progress with the share of the period elapsed (the tick on each bar). Behind = more than 25 points short, at risk = 10–25.
  • Milestone: verified-done tasks ÷ all its tasks, weighted by effort. "Verified" means its PR merged, its post is live or its owner attached proof.
  • Workstream: its outcome metric's trend plus counts of open, blocked and overdue tasks. No blended score.

6 · The task

One model for every piece of growth work

Fields

  • Title, description, workstream, type (page, content, social post, store listing, experiment, partner, fix, research)
  • Why: evidence text, linked facts and charts, source (AI analysis of a date, or a person)
  • Priority P0–P3 from impact × confidence ÷ effort, overridable with a reason
  • Expected impact, effort (hours), owner, reviewer, due date
  • Links: page, keyword, campaign, partner, pull request, post URL
  • Success metric: metric, baseline, target, check date
  • Result: actual value, verdict (worked / no change / worse / too early), learning
  • Goal and milestone it serves; comments; full history

One lifecycle (replaces three)

Suggested→Ready→In progress→In review→Done→Measured

Plus Blocked (with the blocker named) and Won't do (with a reason the agent learns from).

  • Suggested: from the agent; someone triages it into Ready or Won't do.
  • In review: a PR, a draft post or a partner offer waits for approval.
  • Measured: the check date passed and the result was written back automatically.

Today's recommendations, proposals and opportunities migrate into this, keeping their history.

7 · AI to execution

8 · Workstreams that don't exist yet

Partnerships, social and the Play Store

Partnerships pipeline

A light CRM: organisation, type (photographer, planner, college, influencer, company), contact, stage (lead → contacted → meeting → pilot → live → won/lost), value, next step and date, owner, notes. Each partner gets its own tagged link, so events, guests and installs per partner are measured automatically. The agent drafts outreach and researches leads; a person sends.

Social & content calendar

A calendar of posts and articles with status (idea, drafted, approved, published). The agent drafts posts from published pages and real milestones; a person posts (APIs later). Every post carries a tagged link, so the calendar shows visits and installs per post.

Play Store (ASO)

Listing text and screenshots as versioned tasks; store listing experiments; ratings and reviews with reply status; store visitors → installs once Play's install reports are readable (one access tick pending). The agent drafts localised listings.

11 · Decisions

What I need from you before W1

1. North-star metric

Weekly backup users (people who backed up at least one photo in the last 7 days), as the strategy proposes. It's the number most tied to value and retention.

2. Targets and their start date

Keep the strategy's day-30 and day-90 targets, counted from the Play production date, and recalibrate after 4 weeks of real data?

3. Team today

Who besides you will use it in the next month (names and roles)? Until then, you're the growth lead and Claude is the agent.

4. Open strategy decisions

Name, launch wedge (events recommended), cost levers, one identity, your hours per week. They'll appear as blocking decisions on the roadmap until made.
